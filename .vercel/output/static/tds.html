Trulioo Design Linter — Custom Figma Plugin

Introducing Automation to the Design Process

My Role

Sole Designer and Developer

Timeline

November 2022 - February 2023

Overview

Trulioo Design Linter (TDS) is a custom design linter that analyzes a project page and displays a list of layers with inconsistencies in color and font within the design system.

TDS Lint is currently released as a Figma plugin in the Product Experience organization and expanded to include all backlogged features.

Opportunity space

Too much reliance on manual processes

The handoff process involves all designs to go through the Visual team for a UI review and quality check before handoff to developers. While effective in catching 80% of errors, it creates a time-consuming burden for the Visual team (Figure 1.0).

The process required extensive manual reviews and quality checks, leading to reduced productivity and an increased risk of errors, as the team spent a significant amount of time manually checking and adjusting designs.

03
Exploration and ideation

Landing on a design linter

From my research, I found that there were several points of opportunity that could be improved along the current design handoff process through automation and design tooling. I decided to focus on creating a custom design linter tool in order to tackle both major themes found from the design team interviews.

Migration to the new design system

A design linter will be able to highlight any design errors resulting from using legacy design systems.

Oversight from designers

A design linter will be able to be used at all points of the design process. Designers will be incentivized to use it early and often to catch errors from inception.

What is a design linter?

A design linter is a software tool that checks files for inconsistencies and adherence to design standards by analyzing designs with predefined rules from the design system.

Feature definition

I mapped the desired experience into four key features:

Error checking

The plugin should be able to output any design inconsistencies within the page.

Analysis by page or selection

Designers will be able to lint through either the entire page or a single selection.

Ignore errors

Designers can remove specific errors from the list if they are an intentional deviation from the design system.

Configuration settings

Designers can adjust linting criteria based on product.

04
Design considerations

Scaling back for an MVP

Although I initially designed for the inclusion of all four key features, I quickly realized when scoping the tool for development timelines, developing the entire application would likely take the better part of a 6 months to a year.

Due to this, I opted to scale back the designs to a MVP version in order to both release the application in an earlier timeframe and gain real user feedback that I could use to improve the next iteration of the design linter (Figure 4.0 and Figure 4.1).

I scaled back the features to the minimum viable features to make the application functional across products, reducing the projected timeline from one year to three months for development.

Development

Sprint planning

Although it was my first time planning engineering sprints, I knew it was important to break down the development effort into multiple tasks rather than tackling the designs all at once.

I invested time into identifying dependencies, prioritizing tasks, and estimating the time required for each task. By focusing on the goals and deliverables needed within every sprint, I was able to ensure a smooth development process (Figure 5.0).

Impact and retrospection

Project impact

At this moment, TDS Lint has been released to the entire design team and is currently being used in the day to day work of the Visual team. The design tool has seen amazing outcomes and great feedback after release!

Results have seen:

Time spent conducting UI reviews reduced by over 80%.

100% reduction in errors overall post-UI review.

Retrospection

During this project, I gained insight into the end-to-end process of creating a product from conception to launch, especially the engineering portion. Going forward, I am able to better communicate with cross-functional teams and improve my own handoff process due to this experience.

In addition, I worked and approached design with an engineering mindset, especially when exploring the technical requirements of the project itself. This mindset is what helped me break down the designs and features through priority and also gave me a taste of what engineers look for and require from design.
