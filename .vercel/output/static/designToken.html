DESIGN TOKENS

Implementing design tokens at State Farm

Overview

I led a multi-team effort to create and implement design tokens throughout the State Farm Design System.

The project aimed to provide a scalable and systematic solution to future-proof the design decisions and visual styles used at State Farm.

Impact

Since the release, design tokens have been adopted by both design and engineering teams, allowing for increased consistency and efficiency in the organization.

I am currently working on expanding design token education and exploring the introduction of multi-brand theming.

01
Background

Issues with visual alignment

Visual style alignment has always been an issue at State Farm, both between and within product teams. While the previous design system had stylesheets, choosing styles was not intuitive and often led to inconsistencies across teams.

Issues with digital alignment

There was no standardized approach to styling. Design and engineering frequently relied on hardcoded values, leading to digital misalignment and inconsistent UI patterns across products.

As a result, foundational changes required manual updates and could take months, if not years, to update.

SYSTEM CHALLENGE

State Farm needed a scalable approach that enabled intuitive style selection while aligning design and engineering around a shared source of truth

02
Deciding on an approach

Landing on design tokens

After conducting research, attending workshops, and speaking with industry experts, it became clear that design tokens could effectively address our issues.

Design tokens are sets of design-related decisions stored in name-value pairs, representing various design elements such as colors, font sizes, or spacing units.

Why design tokens?

Design tokens target both issues, semantic naming structures inform the user when to use a token, and platform agnostically allows engineering and design to share the same language.

03
Systemizing the design decisions

I wanted to avoid over-engineering our design tokens, especially since the concept was new to State Farm.

Two-layer token structure

I determined that a two-layer token structure would be sufficient to ensure clarity while still enabling us to provide semantic naming for our users.

Although a three-layer token system could facilitate component tokens and support a multi-brand experience, the potential advantages could complicate the system beyond what most teams would require.

Three-part naming convention

I focused on creating a naming structure that was easy to understand and scalable enough to accommodate future token additions or changes. Each name should clearly describe where and when to use each token.

To achieve this, I developed a three-part naming structure consisting of property, role, and modifiers.
With the design token structure in place, I was able to effectively tokenize the design decisions within the system.

04
Validating the structure

Once the initial token set was complete, I conducted user research with the practice in order to ensure that the design tokens would meet the needs and requirements of the product teams within the organization

Simplifying token categories

Initially, tokens were categorized into very specific use-cases, such as separating spacing into padding and gap, despite their scales being identical.

However, testing uncovered that users preferred simplicity during token selection rather than having every case abstracted into its own category.

Building in accessible design

User testing also enabled us to identify edge cases where designers might inadvertently create non-accessible designs, particularly involving color contrast.

We were able to catch these instances and ensure that the available color tokens would guarantee 100% color contrast compliance, provided they are used in the appropriate use-cases.

Building out education

Because design tokens were a new concept for our designers, we received numerous requests for in-depth token education.

I led several educational initiatives, including creating recorded Figma demos, hosting office hours and share sessions, and developing tools to assist designers in selecting tokens.

05
Implementation on the backend

Design implementation

The Gitlab repository is synchronized with the Figma variables via a custom Figma plugin.

Engineering implementation

The tokens are processed and packaged using a CD/CI process that utilizes Style dictionary to convert JSON to a usable format.

Managing a single source of truth across changes

I worked collaboratively with our engineers to develop a custom Figma plugin that helps maintain GitLab as the single source of truth.

The plugin can detect any discrepancies between the tokens JSON file and our variable set within Figma. Once a difference is identified, the user has the option to either revert the change or push the update to GitLab, where it will trigger a pipeline to update the tokens accordingly.

06
Impact

Improved cross-team alignment through the usage of a shared language

By standardizing the way tokens are defined and implemented, teams can easily collaborate, ensuring that design choices are cohesive and aligned with brand standards.

Time required to change design styles reduced by 93%

The time it takes to change style values at State Farm has been drastically reduced from several months to just a few minutes.
